Research

My work lies at the intersection of psychology, epistemology and law. I study how scientific knowledge is used in society, and how it can improve decision-making in the legal system.

Much of it concerns psychopathy: what the research actually shows, and whether psychopathy assessments belong in court. I also work on applied ontology in psychiatry, and, with David Sackris, on the nature of moral and aesthetic judgment. My research has appeared in The Lancet Psychiatry, Psychology, Public Policy, and Law and Philosophical Psychology.

Book

Psychopathy Unmasked

The Rise and Fall of a Dangerous Diagnosis

Psychopathy assessments inform sentencing, parole and transfer decisions across North America. The book surveys the research and argues that people labelled psychopaths are not meaningfully more dangerous than, or psychologically different from, other offenders, and that the legal use of these assessments should end.

The MIT Press, 2025. 328 pp. ISBN 978-0-262-55220-2

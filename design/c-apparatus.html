I work on how scientific knowledge is produced, contested and put to use: in psychiatry, in the courtroom, and in the ontologies that structure scientific data.1 Much of my research examines psychopathy, a diagnosis widely used in the legal system whose scientific basis does not hold up to scrutiny.2 My book argues that psychopathy assessments should no longer be used in court.3

The book

Psychopathy Unmasked

The Rise and Fall of a Dangerous Diagnosis

Psychopathy assessments inform sentencing, parole and transfer decisions for an estimated hundreds of thousands of offenders in North America each year. The book surveys the research and finds that people labelled psychopaths are not meaningfully more dangerous than, or psychologically different from, other offenders. On that evidence, it argues that the legal use of psychopathy assessments should end.

Expertise

Law and personality disorders. Guidance for legal teams on psychopathy and antisocial personality disorder, including how to respond to expert evidence that presents these labels as diagnoses with fixed prognoses, such as high risk or untreatability.

Talks and lectures. Talks on science and society, forensic psychology and epistemology, prepared for the audience at hand: courts and bar associations, universities, or the public.

Ontology and taxonomy. Ontology development for structuring data in the health and cognitive sciences, grounded in Basic Formal Ontology and work on OBO Foundry projects.

About

Rasmus Rosenberg Larsen is Associate Professor of Forensic Epistemology and Philosophy of Science at the University of Toronto Mississauga, and an Affiliated Scientist at the National Center for Ontological Research. His research appears in The Lancet Psychiatry, Psychology, Public Policy, and Law, and Philosophical Psychology.

He holds a PhD in philosophy of science from the University at Buffalo. His work is driven by a commitment to improving how scientific knowledge is used in society, particularly in the legal system.

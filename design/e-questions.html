What I work on

Open a question for a short answer and the research behind it.

Psychopathy assessments are routinely presented in court as evidence of high risk and untreatability. A systematic review of the empirical literature found that these claims are not well supported by the evidence.

The book Psychopathy Unmasked extends the argument and concludes that psychopathy assessments should not be used in the legal system.

Read

  • Are PCL psychopaths dangerous, untreatable, and without conscience?Psychology, Public Policy, and Law, 2020
  • Psychopathy treatment and the stigma of yesterday's researchKennedy Institute of Ethics Journal, 2019
  • Psychopathy UnmaskedThe MIT Press, 2025

Neuroimaging studies of psychopathy are increasingly cited in legal settings. Using fMRI research on psychopathy as a case study, this work argues that unreproducible neuroimaging findings should be kept out of court, and examines how coding choices can create the appearance of a distinctive “psychopathic brain”.

Read

  • How to keep unreproducible neuroimaging evidence out of courtPsychology, Public Policy, and Law, 2023
  • Is the psychopathic brain an artefact of coding bias?Frontiers in Psychology, 2021

How do forensic practitioners actually use psychopathy assessments, and what ethical obligations follow from the scientific uncertainty around them? This work combines a survey of Canadian practitioners with an ethical analysis from the standpoint of forensic mental health.

Read

  • Are psychopathy assessments ethical? A view from forensic mental healthJournal of Threat Assessment and Management, 2022
  • Psychopathy assessments in forensic psychiatry: A pilot study of Canadian practitionersJournal of Criminological Research, Policy and Practice, 2022

Psychiatric knowledge is split across paradigms that rarely talk to each other. This work proposes applied ontology, a formal method for structuring scientific data, as a way to integrate phenomenological descriptions of patient experience with clinical and affective science.

Read

  • More phenomenology in psychiatry? Applied ontology as a method towards integrationThe Lancet Psychiatry, 2022
  • Mapping the patient's experiencePhenomenology and Mind, 2020
  • From affective science to psychiatric disorder: Ontology as semantic bridgeFrontiers in Psychiatry, 2018

Philosophers and psychologists often treat moral and aesthetic judgment as unified kinds. Drawing on empirical evidence, this work with David Sackris questions whether either category picks out a single, distinctive type of judgment.

Read

  • The disunity of moral judgment: Evidence and implicationsPhilosophical Psychology, 2024
  • Are there “moral” judgments?European Journal of Analytic Philosophy, 2023
  • Are there “aesthetic” judgments?Erkenntnis, 2023
